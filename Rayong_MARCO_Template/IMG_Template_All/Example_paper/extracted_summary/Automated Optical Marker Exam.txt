Abstract— This paper presents an automated system for 
evaluating optical marker-based multiple-choice answer sheets 
using images captured from smartphone cameras. The proposed 
approach addresses the challenges of document distortion, 
varying lighting conditions, and diverse sheet templates through 
a multi-stage pipeline. The system begins with image dewarping 
using homographic transformation to correct perspective 
distortions. Key structural components—such as Exam 
Number, Student ID, and Page Number—are localized using 
YOLOv8 object detection. The answer region is then identified 
and cropped for analysis. Template matching is employed to 
detect landmark points, enabling precise alignment of the 
answer grid. Optical Mark Recognition (OMR) is performed 
using a Cross-Linear Area (CLA) algorithm, which calculates 
the ratio of dark pixels within each marker region to determine 
marked 
responses. 
Detected 
answers 
are 
mapped 
to 
corresponding questions and exported in JSON format for 
downstream use. Experimental results demonstrate that the 
system is accurate, efficient, and robust to real-world imaging 
conditions, offering a practical solution for low-cost, mobile-
based exam evaluation. 
Keywords—component, formatting, style, styling, insert (key 
words) 
I. INTRODUCTION
The automation of examination grading processes has 
become increasingly vital in modern educational and 
certification systems, aiming to reduce human error, minimize 
processing time, and enhance overall efficiency. Traditional 
Optical Mark Recognition (OMR) technologies, while 
effective, typically depend on specialized scanning equipment 
and standardized answer sheets, leading to significant 
infrastructural costs and limited adaptability in diverse 
operational environments [1]. In recent years, the proliferation 
of high-resolution smartphone cameras has opened new 
avenues for developing low-cost, portable OMR solutions 
accessible to a broader range of users [2]. 
This paper presents an automated optical marker exam 
evaluation system utilizing a novel Cross-Linear Area (CLA) 
algorithm, designed specifically for smartphone-based image 
acquisition. In contrast to conventional OMR techniques, 
which primarily rely on pixel intensity analysis, thresholding, 
or simple contour detection, the proposed CLA algorithm 
evaluates the distribution of filled areas across predefined 
cross-linear scan paths. This method enhances detection 
robustness against common challenges such as variable 
illumination, non-uniform shading, form distortions, and 
imprecise user markings, which frequently impair the 
reliability of traditional approaches. 
The proposed system enables users to capture examination 
sheets using standard smartphone cameras without requiring 
extensive alignment procedures or highly controlled lighting 
conditions. Subsequent processing steps involve form 
normalization, marker localization, and response recognition 
based on the CLA algorithm, culminating in automatic 
scoring. Experimental validation across various smartphone 
models, lighting scenarios, and form designs demonstrates the 
system's effectiveness and resilience, achieving high accuracy 
comparable to that of conventional OMR systems while 
offering greater flexibility and significantly reduced 
operational constraints. 
II. PROBLEM STATEMENTS
 Despite the widespread adoption of Optical Mark 
Recognition (OMR) systems for automated exam grading, 
traditional OMR solutions are inherently constrained by 
several critical limitations [3]. Firstly, they require specialized 
hardware such as high-precision scanners and uniformly 
printed answer sheets, resulting in high deployment and 
operational costs. Secondly, conventional OMR techniques 
exhibit sensitivity to variations in environmental conditions, 
including uneven lighting, paper distortions, and incomplete 
or ambiguous markings, which can significantly degrade 
recognition accuracy [4]. These constraints make traditional 
systems less accessible, particularly in resource-limited 
settings such as remote educational institutions or informal 
testing environments [5]. 
The advent of smartphone technology, with its ubiquitous 
availability and continual advancements in camera quality, 
presents an opportunity to democratize automated exam 
evaluation by eliminating the dependency on specialized 
equipment [6]. However, leveraging smartphone-captured 
images introduces new technical challenges, including 
variable lighting, perspective distortions, occlusions, and 
inconsistent answer sheet formats. Traditional threshold-

III. BACKGROUND 
Before Optical Mark Recognition (OMR) is a widely used 
technique for the automated reading of marked data from 
paper forms, particularly in standardized testing and survey 
collection. Traditional OMR systems typically rely on high-
resolution scanners and fixed-form layouts, which require 
precise printing and alignment. These systems, while accurate, 
are costly, infrastructure-dependent, and lack portability—
posing limitations in decentralized or resource-limited 
educational settings [7]. 
With the increasing availability and performance of 
smartphone cameras, mobile-based OMR systems have 
gained attention as cost-effective alternatives. However, such 
systems must overcome several challenges, including 
variations in lighting, perspective distortion, and inconsistent 
user handling [8]. To address these challenges, recent work 
has incorporated computer vision and deep learning methods 
to enhance document capture robustness and response 
recognition. 
Object detection models such as YOLO (You Only Look 
Once) have shown strong performance in localizing objects in 
real-time applications. YOLOv8, the latest iteration, offers 
improved accuracy and speed [9], making it suitable for 
mobile deployments. Additionally, template matching 
remains a reliable method for detecting fixed visual features, 
such as alignment markers, when geometric consistency is 
required. 
This work builds upon these advancements by integrating 
image

YOLOv8-based 
field 
localization, 
template-based landmark detection, and a custom Cross-
Linear Area (CLA) algorithm to enable reliable, end-to-end 
OMR processing directly from smartphone-captured images.

The proposed Automated Optical Marker Exam 
Evaluation System is designed to operate end-to-end from 
image acquisition to structured output generation, leveraging 
standard smartphone cameras and the novel Cross-Linear 
Area (CLA) algorithm for robust mark detection. The

A.  Image Acquisition 
     The examination answer sheet is captured using a 
smartphone camera. To enhance robustness against 
environmental variability, users are guided to position the 
sheet within a visible frame, but strict alignment is not 
mandatory. Captured images are stored at high resolution to 
preserve detail necessary for subsequent processing.

systematically scanned along multiple linear cross-sections, 
and the density of dark pixels along these paths is analyzed.

shading and partial markings, offering superior robustness

VI. CONCLUSION AND DISCUSSION 
     This paper presented a smartphone-based automated 
system for evaluating multiple-choice exam answer sheets 
using a combination of modern computer vision techniques, 
including YOLOv8 object detection, template matching, and 
a Cross-Linear Area (CLA) algorithm for Optical Mark 
Recognition (OMR). The proposed system effectively 
addresses common challenges in mobile-based document 
processing, such as perspective distortion, template 
variability, and inconsistent lighting. Experimental results on 
a diverse dataset of answer sheets demonstrated high 
accuracy in field detection and answer recognition, as well as 
fast processing speeds suitable for real-time use on mid-range 
mobile devices. The modular architecture, comprising 
dewarping, component localization, answer region detection, 
landmark alignment, and OMR, enables the system to be 
easily adapted to different templates and deployment 
contexts. Despite the strong performance, the system has 
some limitations. Recognition accuracy may be affected by 
heavily smudged or partially filled markers, and performance 
can degrade under extreme lighting conditions or severe 
image blur. Future work will focus on integrating adaptive 
illumination correction, real-time feedback during capture, 
and end-to-end deep learning approaches for bubble detection 
to 
further 
improve 
robustness 
and 
usability. 
     In conclusion, the system offers a low-cost, portable, and 
scalable solution for automated exam evaluation, particularly 
valuable in resource-constrained educational environments 
where traditional OMR scanners are impractical or 
unavailable.
